# The rail on `/v1`: the second half of "Both"

- **Date:** 2026-10-24
- **Request, verbatim.** Asked how a merchant should refund a routed charge
  (the gap the v0.12.0 docs re-read found), the maintainer answered
  **"Both"**: the refund route accepts the charge's method type, _and_ `/v1`
  shows the rail.
- **The first half** is `POST /v1/refunds` accepting
  `destination[<method type>][…]` beside `destination[<rail code>][…]`
  (vaam-apps/vpay#363,
  [verification](../status/verification/2026-10-24-refund-by-method-type.md)).
- **The decision, verbatim.** This page was drafted as a plan with one shape
  decision for the maintainer (§ "Decisions"), and the answer was **"Go with
  A"** (2026-10-24). The other three decisions took their defaults.
- **Status: built, in the change that carries this page,** except what § "Not
  built" lists. The evidence is
  [the verification page](../status/verification/2026-10-24-rail-on-v1.md).
  Nothing in it has run against a real rail, and its container-backed cases
  had not run when it was written: CI's `rust` job is their first run.

## What was true before

- A charge records its rail (`charges.provider_code`) and, since migration
  `0053`, its method type (`charges.method_type`): set on every routed charge
  and on a rail-code charge whose rail serves exactly one type; absent on a
  rail-code charge on a rail serving several (`notchpay`) and on charges
  written before `0053`.
- The routing decision is a `routing_decisions` row: the type, every rail
  considered with its exclusion reason or score, the chosen and pinned rails,
  the formula and a configuration version.
- **Neither was on any `/v1` object.** `/v1` has no charge object; the
  `payment_intent` showed `payment_method_types` (what the intent _may_ be
  confirmed against) and nothing about what it _was_ confirmed against.
  [ADR-0029](../adr/0029-card-and-bank-adapters-and-payment-method-routing.md)
  D10 said the decision is recorded "never on the `/v1` object".
- **One charge per intent, forever** (ADR-0029 D11), so "the intent's rail"
  is one value, never a list.
- **Every surface that renders an intent goes through one function**,
  `PaymentIntentObject::try_from(&PaymentIntentRow)`: the `/v1` retrieve,
  list, confirm and cancel; the browser's checkout-session expansion; and the
  worker's `intent_snapshot`, which writes `events.data.object` for every
  `payment_intent.*` webhook. Only `requires_action` read the charge, for
  `next_action`.

## The shape (A, chosen): `payment_method_details` on the intent

```json
"payment_method_details": null
"payment_method_details": { "type": "mtn_wallet", "rail": "mtn_momo" }
"payment_method_details": { "type": null, "rail": "notchpay" }
```

- `null` until the intent has a charge; then always an object.
- `rail` is the code of the rail the charge was made on. It is never `null`
  inside the object.
- `type` is the charge's recorded method type, or `null` where the charge
  records none (a rail-code charge on a rail serving several types, or a
  charge from before `0053`).
- The name is Stripe's, from its `Charge` object, where it is
  `{ "type": …, "<type>": { … } }`. A flat `rail` beside `type` leaves room
  for ADR-0029 D12's per-type attributes later (`card.brand`) without
  breaking a decoder: `{ "type": "card", "rail": "…", "card": { "brand": … } }`.
- It is the vocabulary the confirm already speaks:
  `payment_method_data[type]` in, `payment_method_options[<type>][rail]` to
  pin, `payment_method_details.{type,rail}` out.

## The source: two columns on the intent, written with the charge

- Migration `0054`: `payment_intents.charge_rail` (a foreign key onto
  `providers`, as `charges.provider_code` is) and
  `payment_intents.charge_method_type` (the same membership CHECK as
  `charges.method_type`, `charge_method_type_is_known`), plus
  `charge_method_type_needs_a_rail`.
- Written by `vpay_db::charges::insert_for_intent` itself, in the transaction
  that inserts the charge, from the row the `INSERT` returned. Every charge
  vpay opens goes through that one function, so no caller can open a charge
  and leave its intent without the pair. It is ADR-0025 D1's shape: a
  compare-and-swap on the intent (`… WHERE id = $1 AND charge_rail IS NULL`),
  keeping `updated_at` current because no trigger does. Never updated
  afterwards: D11's one charge per intent makes the pair immutable.
- **The backfill copies, it does not infer.** The migration sets each
  existing intent's pair from its charge exactly: `charge_method_type =
charges.method_type`, which is `NULL` for a charge written before `0053`,
  even on a rail serving one type. _(This draft said a pre-`0053` charge on a
  single-type rail "gets that type (`mtn_momo` → `mtn_wallet`)". Corrected
  before it was built: that would make the intent say something its charge
  does not, breaking the invariant this page relies on — the intent's pair is
  its charge's — and `0053` chose `NULL` as "the truth about a charge whose
  network vpay never recorded".)_ `updated_at` is left alone by the backfill:
  it changes no fact about an intent.
- **Why not read the charge at render time:** a `PaymentIntentRow` is built
  by 14 statements in `payment_intents.rs`, four of them
  `UPDATE … RETURNING {COLUMNS}` (measured 2026-10-24), and the worker's
  snapshot and settlement reach it through those. A join would have to reach
  each, and a `RETURNING` clause cannot join without a sub-select. Two columns
  on the table only lengthen `{COLUMNS}`. They also make the worker's event
  snapshot true at the moment it was taken. _(The draft also counted "two
  `UPDATE … RETURNING` statements in `settlement.rs`". Those return the
  charge's columns, not the intent's; corrected.)_
- **The cost:** the rail is stored twice. Integration cases assert the
  intent's pair equals its charge's on every confirm path, and
  `postgres_smoke`'s drift count moves by three (predicted, then measured in
  CI: 212), because `model PaymentIntent` was not extended.

## What stays off `/v1`

The decision itself: the candidates, their scores and exclusion reasons, the
formula and the configuration version stay in `routing_decisions`. D10 is
amended, not reversed: **the outcome (type and rail) is on `/v1`; the
decision record is not.**

## Decisions for the maintainer, and the answers

1. **The shape.** **A (chosen, "Go with A"):**
   `payment_method_details: null | { type, rail }` on the intent, as above.
   B was two top-level keys, `rail` and `payment_method_type`. C was
   Stripe's own placement: `latest_charge` plus a `GET /v1/charges/{id}`.
2. **D10's amendment**, worded as above. Default taken; ADR-0029 carries it.
3. **The payer's surface.** The browser's checkout-session expansion and
   `/v1/browser` render the same object, so the payer sees the rail too.
   Default taken: the payer is already shown the rail's own flow, and a rail
   code is not personal data (`schemas/privacy-inventory.yaml` classifies
   both columns as system configuration, as the charge's own two are).
4. **Past webhooks.** `events.data` is the snapshot at the time and is not
   rewritten, so an old event has no key. Default taken. The SDKs model the
   key as optional for that reason, as they do `customer`.

## What was built

1. **Database:** `0054` (columns, CHECKs, backfill, comments, and a corrected
   `COMMENT ON TABLE routing_decisions`), `MANIFEST.sha256`, the privacy
   inventory, `postgres_smoke`'s drift count.
2. **Write path:** `insert_for_intent` copies rail and type onto the intent.
3. **Read path:** `PaymentIntentRow` and `PaymentIntentObject` gain the
   field; `every_documented_key_is_present_including_the_null_ones` went
   from thirteen keys to fourteen, and so did the prose and the integration
   asserts that counted this object.
4. **SDKs:** Rust `PaymentMethodDetails` and `PaymentIntent`'s field, with
   its fixture; Node `types.ts`; `@vaam-apps/vpay-stripe-js`'s types and
   samples; the Tauri guest-js fixture. Each optional, so an older vpay still
   decodes. A stripe-compat case asserts stripe-node passes the key through.
5. **Docs:** `docs/api/README.md`, ADR-0029 D10, the method-type-routing
   plan, `docs/sdks/parity.md`, the verification page, `docs/status/`.
6. **Skills:** in vaam-apps/vpay-skills, the pages that said the rail is on
   no `/v1` object.

## Not built

- **The Flutter checkout plugin** does not model the key. Its `fromJson`
  reads named keys, so it decodes the object and ignores this one; no Dart
  toolchain was available to change and test it.
- **A `/v1/charges` route** (shape C). Not chosen.
- **The rail on the `refund` object.** A refund names its intent, and the
  intent names the rail.
- **`routing_decisions` in any form**, including on the dashboard.
- **Typing old `notchpay` charges** by the network the payer chose at Notch
  Pay: vpay does not record it, so `type` stays `null` for them.
