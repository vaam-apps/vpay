# 2026-10-22 — RFC-0009 revised: one company first, Stripe Connect's shape later

Branch `claude/happy-hopper-r3h6rz`, from `master` at `3eee006` (v0.10.3).
**Documentation only. No source file, route, migration, gate or
`NotImplemented` token moved, and nothing in RFC-0009 is built.**

## What changed, and on whose decision

The maintainer decided, in the running session's chat:

- the merchants served for now are **"Same company for now"**;
- for later: **"I prefer the way Stripes does the connect already"**. Choosing
  among proposed options, the maintainer picked: both layers, phased; direct
  charges plus an application fee; accounts in the database, secrets too; the
  operator as the only platform;
- webhooks are configured per account: **"Shouldn't webhook be configured by
  account ?"**;
- onboarding is its own application: **"Hosted page for onboarding should be
  a separate application again, like the checkout"**.

[RFC-0009](../../rfc/0009-provider-accounts-per-merchant.md) now has two
phases:

- **Phase A, now.** `deployment.legal_entity`, a `legal_entity` on each
  merchant, a boot refusal for a second company, and
  `charges.provider_account_id`, recorded as `default` on every charge.
- **Phase B, designed and not scheduled.** Connected accounts (`acct_…` rows),
  the operator's platform client and the `Stripe-Account` header, a separate
  onboarding application, application fees, per-account callbacks and sealed
  secrets in the database.

Sections 1 to 5 keep their numbers, because RFC-0010 and RFC-0011 cite § 1
and RFC-0013 cites §§ 3 and 4. The original "Fail closed on two", "Onboarding stays a PR" and
delivery table are struck through or quoted, with dated notes.

## The measurement the revision rests on

RFC-0009's "What the tree does" was re-measured on `master` at `3eee006`:

- no Rust source names a provider account or a legal entity. `grep` for
  `provider_account` finds only the failure code `provider_account_blocked`;
- `MerchantClient`'s fields are unchanged: `client_id`, `merchant_id`, `jwks`,
  `grant_types`, `scopes`, `allowed_audiences`, `client_secret`, `webhooks`,
  `publishable_keys`, `display_name`, `checkout_origins` and `invoices`;
- migrations `0001` to `0050` add no column for an account. `charges`
  carries `provider_code` (`0004`) and nothing narrower.

## Corrections made on the way

- **`docs/status/backend.md`.** "Run one deployment per merchant" now carries
  a dated note: the rule is one deployment per legal entity. Several projects
  of one company may share one, and a merchant that is another company, a
  subsidiary included, still needs its own. The note says that nothing in code
  enforces this until phase A is built, and that counsel has not been asked.
- **`docs/rfc/0001-settlement-and-payouts.md`.** Its 2026-10-16 note on the
  gap gains the same distinction.
- **`docs/flows/adapter-notchpay.md`** § Preconditions. "A deployment serves
  one merchant" gains the same distinction, and points at RFC-0012 § 10 item
  10: whether a company may hold a Notch Pay account at all.
- **`docs/rfc/README.md`.** RFC-0009's status names the two phases. RFC-0012's
  row said "not buildable until ADR-0029 D1's third rule is resolved", which
  has been untrue since ADR-0031 on 2026-10-20. It now says so, with a dated
  note.

## What the revision does not claim

- **That phase A is legal.** The maintainer's reading is that a company
  collecting its own projects' revenue into its own accounts needs no status.
  The RFC records that this reading is not counsel's (open question 5).
- **That application fees are allowed.** § 9 says they conflict with ADR-0029
  D1 rule 1 as written, and need an amending ADR and counsel before B5.
- **Anything about Stripe or Notch Pay Sync beyond what the RFCs already
  cite.** Whether Notch Pay Sync can take an application fee is open question
  9, for Notch Pay.
